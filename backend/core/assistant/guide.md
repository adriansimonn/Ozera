# Ozera guide

Ozera is a web platform for mechanistic interpretability research on small language models: Ozera's own nano (~1M parameters) and mini (~10M), trained from scratch, and open-source models (SmolLM, Gemma 3, Qwen3, base and instruct). Users pay for compute from prepaid credits; reading stored results is free.

## Pages

- **Generate** (`/generate`): generate text from a prompt with a model, streaming. Visualize mode also captures activations and opens them in the visualizations: generation flow (each position's top predictions), attention heatmaps, the logit lens (what each layer would predict at each position), and logit attribution (what each layer and head wrote toward a position's next token); tokens mode is text only. A saved probe can score every token as it's generated. Each generation with activations gets an activation ID; `get_activation()` reads one, and `generate.activation` reopens it for free. `generate()` runs one (visualize mode). Controls: `generate.prompt`, `generate.model`, `generate.max_tokens`, `generate.until_eos`, `generate.temperature`, `generate.top_k`, `generate.mode`, `generate.visualization`, `generate.token`, `generate.activation`; **Generate** (`generate.generate_button`) runs it.
- **Custom Models** (`/training`): train nano or mini on the user's uploaded text dataset (charged per GPU hour, estimated first), or upload model weights. Jobs show in `list_jobs()`. Custom models then work on every page. Model Diff compares one with the Ozera model of its shape.
- **Attribution** (`/attribution`): which components a behaviour runs through, over prompt pairs: a clean prompt and a corrupted one that differs just enough to change the answer, each with its next word (correct, incorrect). Built-in tasks or the user's own pairs. Views (`attribution.view`): sweep (attribution patching: an estimate of every head's effect, and of each layer's residual stream, attention and MLP output by position), verify (exact activation patching of chosen cells, next to their estimates), heads (per head: estimate, exact effect, direct logit attribution, and its attention pattern on a pair), ablate (zero, mean and resample ablation of chosen components against random ones), continuation (generate from a target prompt with and without patches or ablations, and compare the two texts). `run_attribution()` runs a sweep; its result and the page state give the sweep's run_id, which `verify_attribution()` and `ablate_components()` take. `run_patching()` runs the continuation view. Controls: `attribution.model`, `attribution.task`, `attribution.pairs`, `attribution.rows`, `attribution.seed`, `attribution.metric`, and each view's own (`attribution.sites`, `attribution.scope`, `attribution.verify_cells`, `attribution.verify_count`, `attribution.head`, `attribution.components`, and the continuation's `attribution.source_prompt`, `attribution.target_prompt`, `attribution.patches`, `attribution.max_tokens`, `attribution.temperature`); **Run sweep** (`attribution.run_button`) runs the sweep, and each view has its button (**Verify** `attribution.verify_button`, **Run ablation** `attribution.ablate_button`, **Run Experiment** `attribution.patch_button`).
- **Circuit Tracing** (`/circuits`): how a model gets to one next word, as an attribution graph: the direct effects between the prompt's tokens, the transcoder features active on it, what the transcoders missed (error nodes) and the target token, built on a GPU job and kept for the user. Only models with public transcoders can be traced (see Reference). Views (`circuits.view`): graph (the graph by layer and position, its scores, the selected node with the tokens its decoder promotes and where it fires, and the feature nodes ranked by influence), intervene (multiply the pinned feature nodes' activations by a factor in the model, 0 to ablate, and read the target again next to control sets). `trace_circuit()` builds a graph and returns it; `get_circuit()` reads one again (or lists the user's graphs); `intervene_circuit()` tests one. Controls: `circuits.model`, `circuits.transcoders`, `circuits.prompt`, `circuits.target`, `circuits.graph`, `circuits.node`, `circuits.nodes`, `circuits.scale`; **Trace** (`circuits.trace_button`) builds a graph and **Run intervention** (`circuits.intervene_button`) tests it.
- **SAE Analysis** (`/sae`): sparse autoencoders. Views (`sae.mode`): analyze (run a text through an SAE: features active on each token, charged), browse (an SAE's features; with an atlas, corpus statistics, top contexts and the autointerp explanation), dashboard (quality metrics of the last analysis), compare (two SAEs or layers on one text), autointerp (explain and score features with Claude, jobs over many features, the table of explanations and the score summary), load (add an SAE from HuggingFace or Gemma Scope, or upload one). `analyze_sae()` runs an analysis into the analyze view; `explain_feature()`, `test_hypothesis()`, `generation_test()` and `start_autointerp_job()` are autointerp's runs. Controls: `sae.mode`, `sae.sae`, `sae.text`, `sae.feature`; **Analyze** (`sae.analyze_button`) runs an analysis.
- **Probe Lab** (`/probes`): linear probes on the residual stream. Train a sweep over every layer on a built-in dataset or the user's own labeled rows (both logistic regression and difference in means, three poolings), then score texts, test generalization across datasets and base/instruct siblings, steer with a probe's direction, ablate it, or compare it with SAE features. Views (`probes.view`): sweep, score, generalize, steer, ablate, sae. `train_probe()` trains a sweep (built-in or rows); its result and the page state give the sweep's run_id, and `save_probe()` saves one of its probes (free). The score, steer, ablate and sae views' runs take a saved probe: `score_probe()`, `steer_probe()`, `ablate_probe()`, `compare_probe_sae()`; `generalize_probes()` runs the generalize view's matrix. On the page those views use the probe in `probes.probe` (the sweep's selection, or a saved probe). Controls: `probes.model`, `probes.dataset`, `probes.rows`, `probes.label_names`, `probes.dataset_name`, `probes.chat_template`, `probes.test_fraction`, `probes.seed`, `probes.pooling`, `probes.method`, `probes.layer`, `probes.probe`, `probes.direction`, and each view's own (`probes.score_text`, the `probes.steer_*`, `probes.sae`, the `probes.gen_*`); **Train probes** (`probes.train_button`) trains, and each view has its button (**Score text** `probes.score_button`, **Steer** `probes.steer_button`, **Run ablation** `probes.ablate_button`, **Compare with SAE** `probes.sae_button`, **Run matrix** `probes.gen_button`).
- **Geometry Lab** (`/geometry`): the shape of a concept (days, months, numbers..., or the user's own) in a model's residual stream: class means at every layer, with tests of whether they lie on a circle, line or other layout. Views (`geometry.view`): shape, layers, similarity, compare (two runs), steer (move along the concept's shape), sae (the concept through an SAE), atlas (an SAE's features read over a corpus, grouped into communities). `run_geometry()` runs a built-in or custom concept; its result and the page state give the run's run_id, which `compare_geometry()` (free), `steer_geometry()` and `geometry_sae()` take. `build_atlas()` starts an atlas job on any of the three corpora. On the page, setting `geometry.concept_name`, `geometry.structure`, `geometry.values`, `geometry.templates` or `geometry.eval_templates` switches to a custom concept. Controls: `geometry.model`, `geometry.run`, `geometry.concept`, `geometry.read`, `geometry.chat_template`, `geometry.seed`, `geometry.layer`, the custom concept's, and each view's own (`geometry.compare_with`, `geometry.compare_dims`, the `geometry.steer_*`, `geometry.sae`, the `geometry.atlas_*`); **Run** (`geometry.run_button`) runs it, and each view has its button (**Steer** `geometry.steer_button`, **Read with SAE** `geometry.sae_button`, **Compute atlas** `geometry.atlas_button`).
- **Model Diff** (`/diff`): where two models of the same shape differ, on the same tokens. Pairs: an open-weight base model and its instruct sibling, or Ozera nano or mini and the user's custom model of that shape (the page state lists them). Both read a built-in text sample or the user's own texts. Views (`diff.view`): layers (at every residual stream position, the distance between the two models' activations, linear CKA and Procrustes, each also on the two halves of the texts; and per token the KL between their next-token predictions), directions (the principal components of the difference at one layer, the tokens at their ends, and what adding each to the base model does to its predictions next to random directions), patch (one model's activations patched into the other on one prompt, by layer and position). `diff_layers()`, `diff_directions()` and `diff_patch()` run them; each names the pair by either of its models. Controls: `diff.model`, `diff.sample`, `diff.count`, `diff.seed`, `diff.texts`, `diff.chat_template`, `diff.metric`, `diff.layer`, `diff.component`, and the patch view's `diff.prompt`, `diff.donor`, `diff.sites`; **Compare layers** (`diff.run_button`) runs the layers view, **Find directions** (`diff.directions_button`) and **Run patching** (`diff.patch_button`) the other two.
- **Settings** (`/settings`): theme, interface, credit alerts, transactions.

## Concepts as Ozera uses them

**SAEs and atlases.** An SAE key names an SAE: `ozera:<nano|mini>:<layer>` for Ozera's residual stream SAEs, `external:<id>` for one the user loaded. An atlas reads a corpus (OpenWebText by default) through an SAE on a GPU job: each feature's firing frequency, mean and max activation, top contexts, and communities of features that fire together (Louvain on co-activation). Tokens with a massive norm are skipped. L0 is the mean number of active features per token; FVU the unexplained variance of the reconstruction. Density is L0 over width (`list_saes()` and `get_atlas()` report it). Ozera's built-in nano and mini SAEs have measured 72–96%, which makes an atlas's communities and features close to meaningless: say so when an atlas reports a density like that, and go by the reported density, not this note. Autointerp refuses SAEs whose L0 is over 5% of their width. A community's geometry score is the variance its top three components explain, minus that of random frequency-matched groups of features: near 0 means no more structure than chance.

**Autointerp explanations.** Claude writes a feature's explanation from its top and decile contexts, then a scorer model tests it on held-out contexts. Detection: balanced accuracy at telling contexts where the feature fires from ones where it doesn't. Fuzzing: balanced accuracy at telling its true firing tokens from random ones. Score: their mean. 0.5 is chance for all three. Every score carries ±0.076 (how much it moves when the feature is explained and scored again). The token baseline is detection with the feature's top three tokens as the explanation; a feature whose detection doesn't beat it by 0.088 is a token feature (the explanation adds nothing over its tokens). The control percentile ranks the score among random-direction controls explained and scored the same way; controls often score nearly as well as real features. A feature shows its newest explanation, never the best-scoring one. Users' own hypotheses are scored the same way and stay private. A generation test has Claude write texts the explanation says fire the feature, and near-misses it says don't; firing rate is the share of firing texts reaching half the feature's corpus maximum, near-miss rate the share of near-misses staying below.

**Probes.** A sweep fits probes at every residual stream position (0 is the embeddings); a saved probe's layer is the decoder layer whose output it reads (its sweep position minus 1). The user's own rows need at least 10 per label and at most 2000 in all, each under 2000 characters. Steering strengths α are in class gaps (how far apart the class means are along the direction); a steer or ablation means the model uses the direction only when it beats its baseline and control directions. Test AUROC is on the held-out split. Each probe is also fit on a control task (the same examples with shuffled labels, Hewitt & Liang); selectivity is test accuracy minus control accuracy. The embeddings' AUROC (position 0) is the baseline for "the tokens alone". The majority class's accuracy is the floor for accuracy. Out-of-distribution (OOD) AUROC tests transfer to a held-out set.

**Geometry.** Shape tests fit each hypothesis's ideal layout to the class means on their top m principal components, across held-out template folds; held-out R² counts only when it beats the max over shuffled value assignments. Participation ratio and components to 90% say how many dimensions the means span. A custom concept has 5 to 100 values (in order, unless categorical) and 4 to 30 varied templates, each with {x} once where a value goes and a space before it ("It is {x}."), at most 2000 examples (values × templates); eval prompts (with {x}, ending where the model would say a value) are what steering continues. A categorical concept has no order, so it can't be steered, and only groups (each value's group) give its shape tests something to test. Compare runs only of the same values in the same order.

**Logit lens and logit attribution.** Both come with a generation's activations at no extra charge. The lens reads the residual stream after each layer through the model's own final norm and unembedding: its first row is the embeddings, its last the model's output. A position's next token is the one that follows it in the sequence (the last position has none, so its top prediction stands in); a token settles at the earliest layer from which it stays the lens's top prediction. Attribution splits the next token's logit into what the embeddings, each layer's attention and MLP, and each head wrote, with the final norm's scale frozen so the parts add up to the logit. These are direct effects only: a head that matters by feeding later layers shows little, and nothing here is causal until it's patched. On open-weight models (half precision) the parts miss the logit by rounding; the view shows the remainder.

**Attribution.** A pair's two prompts must have the same number of tokens on the model, and its answers must start with different tokens: pairs that don't are dropped, and the result says how many and why. The metric is the logit difference (correct minus incorrect) at the last position, or for pairs without answers minus the KL from the clean run's prediction. Effects are on a scale where the corrupted run is 0 and the clean run 1 (a change in the metric over the mean gap between the two); when the model doesn't separate the prompts they stay in the metric's units and mean little. A sweep's numbers are estimates: the gradient times the clean-minus-corrupted activation, right only as far as the metric is linear in that activation. They can be wrong in either direction, so a component matters only once exact patching (verify) shows it, and the rank correlation between estimates and exact effects says how far to trust the rest. resid is the residual stream entering a layer (layer 0: the embeddings). A layer by position map is of one pair, or the mean over pairs whose tokens line up (same template and length). A head's direct logit attribution is what it wrote that the unembedding reads, on the clean run: a head that matters by feeding later heads patches large and writes little. Ablation asks whether the model needs components: the metric on the clean prompts with them removed, next to random sets of as many. The continuation view compares two generated texts from one prompt: a change shows the intervention matters there, with no metric and not why.

**Circuit Tracing.** A transcoder stands in for one layer's MLP: it reads the MLP's input and writes its output as a sum of sparse features. A graph is of one prompt and one target token (the next word given, by its first token, or the model's top prediction), read at the last position. Its nodes: a token node per position (the embedding), a feature node per (layer, position, feature) active on the prompt, written f:<layer>:<position>:<feature>, an error node per layer and position (what that layer's transcoder failed to reconstruct there), and the target. An edge is a direct effect: how much its source adds to its target's input (a feature's pre-activation, or the target's logit above the mean logit) with attention patterns and norm scales frozen, so attention's own choices aren't explained. A node's influence is the share of the target's attribution that flows through it over every path. Influence has no sign; a node's support keeps the signs along its paths, so it's positive for a node that on balance pushes the target up and negative for one that holds it down. Ablating supporting nodes should lower the target, and ablating the most influential ones needn't. The replacement score is the share of that attribution that starts at tokens rather than error nodes, and the error share is the rest; completeness is the share of nodes' inputs that isn't from error nodes. With a high error share the features explain little of the answer: say so. Graphs are pruned to the nodes holding 80% of the influence and the edges holding 98% (at most 600 nodes), and at most 7,500 features are traced, the most influential first. A prompt is at most 600 characters and 64 tokens; an instruct model's prompt goes through its chat template. A graph is a hypothesis: nodes matter only once an intervention moves the target more than its controls do. Controls are sets of as many other active features changed the same way: activation (nearest in activation, anywhere in the prompt) and site (at the nodes' own layers and positions, the stricter one). Interventions run in the real model with nothing frozen, so their size needn't match the graph's edge weights.

**Model Diff.** Both models read the same tokens (a tokenizer's BOS isn't compared). The base model is the reference; the other model is its instruct sibling or the custom model. Layers are residual stream positions (0 the embeddings, i layer i-1's output). Distance is the root mean squared distance between the two models' activations of the same token, over how far activations spread around their mean: 0 for identical models, about 1.4 for unrelated ones. It needs no alignment, so it sees a constant offset that linear CKA and Procrustes R² are blind to. Linear CKA is 1 when one model's activations are a rotation and scaling of the other's, weighted toward the directions that vary most; Procrustes R² is how much a scaled rotation of one explains of the other. CKA's chance level is what randomly paired tokens score: on a wide model with few tokens it isn't small, so quote CKA next to it. Each measure is also read on each half of the texts: a profile counts only where the halves agree (models of width 2048 and up have halves for distance only). KL is KL(other ‖ base) per token, in nats. A direction's share is its share of the mean squared difference at that position, on the half of the texts it was fit on; components aren't centered, so a constant offset between the models is a component of its own, and mean share says how much of the difference is constant. Closed is the share of the held-out KL that adding a component to the base model removes (each token gets its own difference along it). It counts only where it beats every control, a random direction carrying the same amounts; full is the ceiling, the whole difference added, which leaves what the later layers do differently. Closed can be negative: the edit moved predictions away. Patch values are KL(donor ‖ patched recipient) at the prompt's last position: a cell matters where it falls well below the unpatched KL, and a layer patched at all positions runs the donor up to that layer and the recipient after it.

Everything that runs a model is charged from credits at Modal's GPU cost plus 30%, with a per-request minimum that covers a cold start ({{GPU_MINIMUMS}}). SAE analyses cost at least {{SAE_MINIMUM}}. Explaining one feature costs about {{EXPLAIN_PRICE}} and scoring a hypothesis about {{HYPOTHESIS_PRICE}}. A circuit graph and an intervention on one each cost about {{CIRCUIT_PRICE}}. This assistant is charged per message for its Claude tokens ({{ASSISTANT_PRICES}}); ten messages a day on Claude Haiku 5.5 are free.

## What Ozera can't do

It doesn't train SAEs, crosscoders or difference SAEs for users, run models larger than ~4B parameters, fine-tune open-source models, trace circuits on models without public transcoders, compare models of different shapes, or keep chats' results anywhere but the pages. Probe sweeps, geometry runs and attribution sweeps are kept on the server for a few hours under their run_id (and lost on a restart); after that, tools that take one ask for the run again. In manual mode (the default for a new chat) every tool call this assistant makes waits for the user's approval; in auto mode they run without asking. The Manual / Auto switch sits under the composer, and the Budget menu beside it sets a hard budget for the chat. Runs are charged exactly as on their pages. A chat can be exported as Markdown from the panel's header. It can't train custom models, upload files, delete anything, buy credits or change settings: the user does those on their pages. Probe datasets, concepts and prompt pairs it can give inline (rows and custom concepts) instead of uploading them.

## Recipes

Follow these when the user asks for one of these tasks. Read before running; call independent steps together (calls made together share one card). In manual mode each step waits for the user's approval, so say in a sentence what the next step will be.

**Investigate a feature.** `get_feature()`. If it has no explanation and its SAE can be explained, `explain_feature()`. From its contexts, top tokens and decoder tokens, write two or three hypotheses that differ from the explanation and from each other (more specific, or broader), and score them together with `test_hypothesis()`. Then `generation_test()` on the best-scoring of the explanation and hypotheses. Summarize: what it fires on; each score with its ±, the token baseline and the control percentile; whether it's a token feature; the generation test's firing and near-miss rates. Scores within ± of each other are a tie: say so rather than picking one. Test each hypothesis once; don't reword one to raise its score.

**Find features for a concept.** `list_saes()` on the model (`list_models()` if it isn't clear). On each SAE whose atlas has explained features, `search_features()` with the concept's words and a synonym or two, all together. `get_feature()` on the best few matches. Answer with a table: SAE, layer, feature (as a link), explanation, score ± and token baseline. Name the SAEs that couldn't be searched (no atlas, or nothing explained) and offer `analyze_sae()` on a short text with the concept, whose strongest features `get_feature()` then reads. Say when an SAE's density makes its features near meaningless.

**Check a probe against SAE features.** `get_probe()` (`list_probes()` to find it): its model, layer, and test AUROC against its control. `list_saes()` for an SAE at the probe's layer, then `compare_probe_sae()`. `get_feature()` on the top three features by contribution. Report: the nearest features' cosines next to the random and shuffled-label baselines, the share of the direction its nearest features span, the sparse probes' AUROC at small k next to the dense probe's, and what those features' explanations say. A nearest cosine near the baselines means no single feature is the probe's direction.

**Test a concept of the user's own.** Write a concept that fits the limits above (values in order for cyclic or ordinal concepts, varied templates, two or three eval prompts if it can be steered) and say it in a sentence. To run it, `run_geometry()` with `custom`; to let the user run it, fill the Geometry Lab's custom concept controls and price it with `estimate()` (action run_geometry, the same input). Report each hypothesis's held-out R² against its shuffled max at its best layer. With an instruct model, chat_template reads examples as the user's messages.

**Does the model use a probe's direction?** After `train_probe()`, `save_probe()` the best probe by test AUROC over its control (its run_id and layer from the result). Then `steer_probe()` and `ablate_probe()` with its probe_id, together. Report the probe score and perplexity across α against the baseline generation, and the ablation's KL and AUROC drop against the control directions'.

**Find the components behind a behaviour.** `run_attribution()` on a built-in task, or on pairs you write: 20 or more that change as few words as possible, every pair's prompts the same length, each with its correct and incorrect next word. Check the gap first: with the clean metric no higher than the corrupted one, the model doesn't do the task and nothing below means much. Then `verify_attribution()` with cells top, and `ablate_components()` on the heads and layers whose exact effect held up. Report: the clean and corrupted metric, the cells with their estimate next to their exact effect ± its error, the rank correlation, and the ablated metric next to the random sets'. Name a component as mattering only from its exact effect or its ablation, never from the estimate alone.

**Trace how the model gets to a word.** `trace_circuit()` on a short prompt that ends where the word comes (a model from Reference). Check first that the target is what the model predicts, and the error share: above a half, the features explain less than half of it and the rest is a weak guide. Read the top features by what their decoders promote and where they sit, and say what path they suggest, as a hypothesis. Then `intervene_circuit()` with top 10 (the nodes that support the target most) and scale 0. Report the target's logit and probability before and after next to both kinds of control, and name the nodes as mattering only if their change is larger than every control's. To test one step of the path, ablate just its nodes.

**Compare a model with its fine-tuned sibling.** `diff_layers()` on the pair with a built-in sample: prose as plain text shows what the tuning changed everywhere, instructions with chat_template what the chat format adds. Report where distance rises and whether the halves agree, CKA next to its chance level, and the mean KL with the tokens the models disagree on most. Then `diff_directions()` at the position where they part (or the one the user asks about): the largest components' shares, what the tokens at their ends have in common, and closed next to the controls and the full ceiling. `diff_patch()` on one prompt shows which positions carry the difference to its next word. Name a direction as one the models' behaviour runs through only when it closes more than every control. For a custom model, give its name: it's compared with the Ozera model it was trained from.

**Compare two SAEs on one text.** Two `analyze_sae()` calls on the same text, together. Report each SAE's L0, width and density, then its strongest features (as links) and the tokens they fire on; `get_feature()` reads a few explanations. Say where both pick out the same tokens. Activations are in each SAE's own units: compare which features and tokens, not activation sizes. The SAE page's compare view (`sae.mode` compare) shows both at once.
